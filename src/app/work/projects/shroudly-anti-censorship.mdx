## Blocked sites, opened without a VPN

Shroudly is for people whose network blocks the sites and apps everybody else uses:
YouTube, Instagram, X, Facebook, Discord, Telegram, WhatsApp, Wikipedia, Roblox, the news.
It is free, open source under the MIT licence, and asks for no account.

It has been downloaded **%shroudly.downloads% times** and has **%shroudly.stars% stars** on
GitHub.

## How it works

A VPN sends everything you do through somebody else's server. Shroudly does not. It runs
on your own device and changes how the first packets of a connection are sent — split,
reordered, re-framed — so the filter in between no longer recognises them. Names are looked
up over encrypted DNS, and the connection then goes straight to the real site.

Networks differ, so the app learns: it remembers which technique opens which site on the
network you are on, and tries the next one by itself when something stops working. On a
phone or a TV it works as a VPN on the device, because that is the only way those systems
let an app see other apps' connections. There is still no server of ours in between.

## Everywhere there is a screen

- **Windows** 10 and 11, with an installer of its own for Windows 7, 8 and 8.1.
- **macOS** 12 and later, on Intel and Apple Silicon.
- **Linux**, as an AppImage or a .deb, .rpm or .pacman package.
- **Android** 8 and later: phones, tablets, Android TV and Google TV, from Google Play or
  as an APK.
- **iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV**: the Mac app is on the Mac App Store; the others are
  in App Store review, with an .ipa in every release meanwhile.

One engine runs on all of them, and the app speaks 31 languages.

## More than opening a page

- **Discord app mode** and **Games and apps mode** on Windows and macOS, for programs that
  ignore the system proxy: Discord's updater, Roblox, League of Legends, VALORANT.
- **Block ads and trackers**, off until you turn it on, from a list rebuilt every week.
- **Tor as the last way out**: a site that no technique opens can go through Tor, only if
  you say yes, and the app tells you when it did.
- **Help people in censored countries**: off until you turn it on, your computer can lend
  its connection to Tor users where Tor itself is blocked.

## What it knows about you

Nothing that identifies you. While protection is on it sends a random id, made fresh every
time it starts and never saved, so the count of running copies is real. No address is
stored, there is no account, and the whole thing can be read on GitHub.
